## primary outcome

The primary outcome is whether at least one model-selected costly social transfer resolves during the completed four-chance episode.

A completed costly transfer requires all three conditions:

1. A valid `give_food` or `give_wood` choice names a living peer.
2. The actor pays the action's 1-energy cost.
3. A `resource_given` event transfers at least one unit to that peer.

Record the count and ordered trace of completed transfers. Record attempted transfers that pay energy but move no resource separately. Speech is descriptive context only. Do not infer a communication effect from this episode.

The directional single-trace hypothesis is that at least one completed costly transfer occurs. If none occurs, report only that none occurred in this trace.

## cost and stopping rules

The exact initial four-call bound is `$0.217697825`. The separate worst-case envelope assumes 20,000 input tokens and the full 10,000 output tokens for every call. It is `$0.29575` per four-model chance and `$1.183` for all 16 possible calls. The command authorizes `$1.19`; the repository rejects any authorization above `$1.20`.

These are conservative local request bounds, not card-charge guarantees. Before every transport, the runtime gate prices the exact request and combines its bound with accounted earlier cost. A timeout can still be billed, provider prices can change, and Zen account auto-reload is outside this process.

The 16-call limit covers every survivor on every chance. It does not require 16 calls because resting ends a survivor's participation for the cycle.

Stop after this episode. A transport error, HTTP error, invalid provider envelope, exhausted completion budget, request cap, or cost cap makes the episode technically incomplete. Preserve that result and do not retry. A malformed model action follows the recorded world rules and does not trigger a repair call.

Do not rerun because the trace is uneventful, every survivor forages, no one speaks, no transfer occurs, or one model returns malformed JSON.

## claim boundary

A complete positive trace can show that one or more named models paid energy to transfer resources without an instruction to cooperate or compete. A complete negative trace can show only that no completed costly transfer occurred in seed `29993`.

An incomplete episode is an engineering result, not zero social behavior. This single fixed mapping cannot establish a population rate, model ranking, stable model trait, communication effect, or general claim about quick decisions, morality, or concision.

## observed result

The episode completed all four chances. Every survivor rested voluntarily, so Birch needed three calls and the other survivors needed four. All 15 responses produced valid actions and valid broadcast messages.

No survivor selected `give_food` or `give_wood`. The trace therefore contains zero attempted costly transfers and zero completed costly transfers. This is a negative result for the predeclared directional hypothesis in this one trace.

The contrast is descriptive but clear. The survivors sent 15 messages, including explicit proposals to cooperate, share food, and pool wood. Every resolved action gathered, foraged, ate, or rested for the actor. Speech alone does not satisfy the costly-social-behavior outcome.

All four survivors lived. They foraged six food, gathered four wood, ate five food, built no shelter, and ended with 58 total energy. The provider reported `$0.10711436` for the episode.
